## Monthly or quarterly: what are you actually paying?

The quarterly option is easy to misread because the pricing cards show a lower monthly equivalent. You are not billed $58 once per quarter for Pro; you pay **$174 upfront for three months**, which works out to $58 per month.

The discount is a straightforward **10%**. There is no need to hunt for a separate promo code just to reach those rates; the lower quarterly equivalent is already shown as part of the plan pricing.

### When monthly billing makes more sense

Monthly billing is the safer call when you are still validating a workflow, a target site, software compatibility, or the number of IPs you can actually use. The Pro plan costs $65 for one month, so the extra $7 versus its quarterly equivalent may be worthwhile if it prevents three months of paying for unused capacity.

This is especially relevant for new projects. A proxy plan can look cheap per IP and still be expensive if 35 of those IPs sit idle.

Monthly billing also fits seasonal work, short data-collection projects, and teams with changing client demand. The per-IP rate is a little higher, but flexibility has a price too. Here, that price is fairly visible rather than hidden behind a maze of usage credits.

### When quarterly billing is the better deal

Quarterly billing makes more sense when you already know you need static US ISP proxies continuously. The discount becomes more meaningful at higher volumes:

- Pro saves $21 over three months.
- Business saves $39 over three months.

The Enterprise plan gets down to **$1.06 per IP per month** on quarterly billing. That is the lowest advertised unit cost, though it only makes financial sense if you genuinely need a 254-IP subnet. Buying 254 addresses to get a better unit price is a bit like renting a warehouse because the shelves were on sale.

## What the price includes, and what it does not promise

A clean comparison needs more than a per-IP figure. HypeProxies publicly lists several shared plan features, but there are also practical limits to keep in mind.

### Included: unlimited bandwidth and threads

Each current ISP tier advertises unlimited bandwidth and unlimited threads. For users running regular data collection or high-volume monitoring, this avoids the common problem of estimating traffic in gigabytes before a project has settled into a predictable pattern.

A fixed bill is helpful, but do not treat “unlimited” as a license to ignore sensible rate controls, target-site terms, or your own infrastructure limits. The right approach is to use only the capacity your legitimate workload requires and monitor your success rates, latency, and error patterns.

### Included: static US ISP IPs

The public product pages position these as static residential or ISP proxies in US locations. “Static” is the key word for many buyers: the assigned address remains stable rather than rotating automatically between requests.

That suits tasks where session continuity matters. It is less suitable when a project specifically needs broad international coverage or a large rotating residential pool. The public residential page mentions a larger network and global locations, but it does not currently show purchasable pricing, so it should not be treated as a directly comparable plan.

### Included: 10 Gbps network positioning

HypeProxies advertises 10 Gbps network access on current plan information, while some product pages describe infrastructure ranges up to 100 Gbps. For a buying decision, the plan-level 10 Gbps claim is the more relevant published baseline.

Actual response time will still depend on the target website, your application, routing, request volume, and where the target is hosted. A proxy network can be fast and still encounter a slow retail site at 3 a.m. on a sale day. The network is only one part of the journey.

### Check protocol requirements before you buy

The provider’s current ISP-focused materials identify **HTTP** support. If your software requires SOCKS5, UDP, a specific API, automatic endpoint rotation, or granular country and city targeting outside the US, verify compatibility before paying for a quarter.

This is the sort of detail that does not look exciting in a pricing table but can decide whether the plan works at all. A cheap plan that does not support your required protocol is not cheap; it is just a support ticket with a subscription attached.

## How to choose the right tier without overbuying

The most useful question is not “Which tier has the lowest per-IP price?” It is “How many IPs will this workload consistently need?”

Use this quick decision process.

1. **Estimate the real concurrent need.**
   Count long-running sessions, tools, client projects, environments, and backups. Do not count every theoretical possibility. A plan should match live operational demand.

2. **Start with Pro if 50 IPs covers the job.**
   Pro is the public entry tier and the lower-risk way to evaluate compatibility on monthly billing.

3. **Choose Business when 100 IPs are genuinely useful.**
   It has a lower per-IP cost than Pro and adds priority support, but it should be driven by capacity needs rather than the temptation of a slightly better unit rate.

4. **Choose Enterprise for subnet-scale operations.**
   A full /24 allocation is appropriate when a large, dedicated pool is a requirement, not a future fantasy spreadsheet.

5. **Use quarterly billing only after confirming fit.**
   The savings are real, but a 10% discount does not compensate for buying the wrong proxy type, wrong location mix, or too many IPs.
